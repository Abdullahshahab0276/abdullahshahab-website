Realtor Logo: What Works for Agents, Teams and Brokerages

How to design a realtor logo that wins listings: styles for agents, teams and brokerages, NAR's REALTOR® logo rules, team name rules and what it costs.

Abdullah ShahabBrand designer · November 25, 2026 · 9 min read

A realtor logo works harder than it looks. It sits on yard signs people read from a moving car, on your Instagram profile at the size of a thumbnail, on a listing flyer next to your brokerage’s logo, and on a closing gift. If it only works on a laptop, it’s not ready.

Short answer: a good realtor logo is a simple name-based mark (a wordmark or monogram, sometimes with a small symbol) in one or two colors, readable on a yard sign and at profile-picture size. Don’t put the REALTOR® block R inside your logo or the word REALTOR in your business name: NAR doesn’t allow it. If you’re on a team, your logo must also sit alongside your brokerage’s name, and in Florida the team name can’t be bigger than the brokerage name.

Do you need a logo as a real estate agent?

Yes, if you want people to remember you and not only your brokerage. Follow Up Boss asks this exact question and answers it the same way: a personal logo helps solo agents, team leaders and independent brokers stand out, even when the brokerage already has one.

Agents change brokerages; your clients and followers stay with you. A personal brand carries that across.

What should a realtor logo include?

Keep it short. Most strong real estate logos are built from three parts:

  • Your name (or team or company name) in a clean, readable font. This is the part people search for later.
  • An optional mark: a monogram, a simple roofline, a key or an abstract shape. Use one, not three.
  • A descriptor, only if it helps: “Real Estate”, “Homes” or your area (“Naples Coastal Homes”).

Leave out your headshot, phone numbers, long taglines and stock house-and-chimney icons. Placester makes the same point: a name-based logo works better for agents than one built around a photo, and too many colors, fonts or symbols make it forgettable.

Which logo style fits an agent, a team or a brokerage?

Who it’s for Style that works Why
Solo agent Wordmark of your name, or a monogram of your initials You are the brand; people search your name
Luxury agent Spaced-out serif wordmark, black, white, cream or gold Quiet, expensive, nothing cartoonish
Team Team name plus a simple symbol that works alone The symbol carries the brand on signs, riders and social
Independent brokerage Symbol plus name, with a full set of versions Needs to scale to offices, agents, cars and ads
Investor or property company Bold geometric mark, strong sans-serif Reads as modern and business-like

Havora (the cover image), a property brand I designed, uses a simple terracotta house mark next to a clean sans-serif wordmark. The house shape is reused as a frame on the listing flyer and as a badge on the agent poster, so the brand is recognizable even when the full name isn’t shown.

What colors and fonts work best for a real estate logo?

Real estate is a trust business, so most of the big names lean on calm, solid colors. Placester describes Compass’s logo as a black-and-white scheme, and The Close points to Keller Williams’ bold red “kw” mark. Both are simple and both work at any size.

A practical way to choose:

  1. Navy, deep green or charcoal for trust and stability.
  2. Black, white and cream, with gold or bronze in small amounts, for luxury listings.
  3. One warm accent (terracotta, coral, orange) if you want to look friendly and different from the navy-and-gold crowd. Havora uses teal and terracotta for exactly that reason.
  4. Two or three colors at most, each with a clear job.

Fonts: serifs feel established and high-end; sans-serifs feel modern. Test either on a yard sign mock-up, because thin scripts disappear from across the street.

Can you use the REALTOR® logo in your own logo?

Most logo guides skip this. The REALTOR® marks belong to the National Association of REALTORS®, and only NAR members may use them. NAR’s rules say:

  • The block R logo goes next to your firm logo, never inside it. NAR’s membership marks manual says the marks are used “adjacent to, but not as a part of” a member’s firm name. Its design rules also say never to combine the R logo with another symbol, frame it, redraw it, tilt it, or use it as the first letter of words like “Realty”.
  • REALTOR can’t be part of your business name. NAR’s firm name guidance gives the example: “Blackacre REALTORS®, Inc.” is improper, while “Blackacre, Inc., REALTORS®” is fine. The term must be separated from the firm name by punctuation.
  • Your logo can’t compete with it. The R logo may sit beside a firm logo as long as that logo doesn’t detract from or compete with it.
  • Color and spacing. NAR recommends REALTOR® Blue (PMS 293) or one sharply contrasting color, and asks for clear space around the R equal to half the width of the block R.

So design your own logo to stand on its own, and place the R logo beside it, at a distance, on cards, signs and ads.

What are the rules for team logos? (Florida example)

Many states have advertising rules for teams. Florida’s is one of the clearest. Under Rule 61J2-10.026, a team is defined as “a name or logo” used by licensees who present themselves as a team, so team logos are covered. The rule says:

  • A team name may include “Team” or “Group”.
  • A team name can’t include words like Realty, Real Estate, Brokerage, Brokers, Company, Associates, Agency, Properties, Inc. or LLC, or anything that suggests the team is a separate brokerage.
  • The team name can’t be printed larger than the registered brokerage name, and people must be able to tell they’re dealing with a team.

Florida Realtors adds that the licensed brokerage name must appear in all real estate advertising, and online it must sit above, below or next to your contact details. So your team logo needs a lockup (a fixed layout) with room for the brokerage name. I design that in from day one.

Outside Florida, check your own state’s real estate commission and your brokerage’s brand rules before you print. This isn’t legal advice.

FAQ

Can I put a house or roof icon in my realtor logo?

You can, but it’s the most common real estate symbol there is. If you use one, make it simple and give it a twist that’s yours, like Havora’s house mark that doubles as a frame for photos.

Can I call myself a realtor in my logo?

Only NAR members can use REALTOR®, and NAR says it must not be part of your business name. Put it after your name or firm name, separated by punctuation, and keep the R logo outside your own logo.

Should my team logo match my brokerage’s brand?

It doesn’t have to match, but it has to sit next to it. Many brokerages have their own brand rules for agent and team logos, so ask for them before you start. In Florida, the team name can’t be larger than the brokerage name.
