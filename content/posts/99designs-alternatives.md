The best 99designs alternative depends on what you want from it. If you like contests, **DesignCrowd** is the closest match. If you want one designer at a low price, look at **Fiverr or Upwork**. For a portfolio-first search, try **Dribbble**. For instant and cheap, an **AI logo maker**. For ongoing design work, a **design subscription**. And if you want a complete identity from one experienced designer at a fixed price, hire an **independent brand designer** directly.

## Why look for a 99designs alternative?

99designs runs logo contests at fixed prices. Its published logo contest tiers range from **$249 to $1,050**, and its full identity packs reach about **$2,499** ([Review42, citing 99designs, July 2026](https://resources.review42.com/how-much-to-charge-for-logo-design/)). Its Logo & brand guide packages run from [$329 to $1,199](https://99designs.com/pricing/logo-brand-guide).

Contests suit founders who want to compare many ideas. They suit you less if you want one person to research your market, explain decisions and stay with the brand after launch. In a contest, most designers are working on spec, and the effort shows in the range of quality.

## 1. DesignCrowd

DesignCrowd works like 99designs: you post a brief and designers submit concepts. Published starting prices vary by source and package, from about [$69](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/get-a-designer/99designs-alternatives) to [$109 for 1–3 designs](https://logodesign.org/my-honest-review-of-designcrowd), rising to $499 for 100+ designs. Same pros and cons as any contest: lots of options, little strategy.

## 2. Fiverr

Fiverr sells fixed "gigs" from thousands of freelancers. You can buy a logo cheaply and quickly, but quality varies enormously, and the cheapest gigs are often template-based. Check whether source files and copyright are included before you order.

## 3. Upwork

Upwork lets you post a job, compare proposals and hire one freelancer under a contract. [Upwork reports](https://www.upwork.com/hire/graphic-designers/cost/) median graphic designer rates of about $15 to $35 an hour. It works well if you're comfortable writing a clear brief and reviewing portfolios.

## 4. Dribbble

Dribbble is a portfolio platform with a services marketplace. You choose a designer by their actual work, which is the most reliable quality signal. Identity packages listed there for startups commonly start from about [$1,411](https://dribbble.com/services/235555-Brand-Identity-Design) to [$4,000](https://dribbble.com/services/175630-Startup-Brand-Identity-Guidelines).

## 5. AI logo makers (Looka, Tailor Brands)

AI tools are the cheapest route: Looka's one-time logo packages are [$35 and $72](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/looka/diffs/2026W38) after a September 2026 price change, and Tailor Brands starts at about $9.99 a month. They're fast, but logos can look generic, and purely AI-generated work can't be registered for copyright in the US. See [AI logo generator vs a human designer](/blog/ai-logo-generator-vs-human-designer/).

## 6. Design subscriptions (ManyPixels, Penji, Kimp)

Subscriptions give you a designer for a monthly fee, about [$699 to $995 a month](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/get-a-designer/99designs-alternatives). They make sense for ongoing work like ads and social posts. For a one-off brand identity, a fixed package is usually better value.

## 7. An independent brand designer

Hiring one designer directly gives you research, a clear process and one person accountable for the result. Prices range widely, so compare the portfolio and the exact deliverables, not only the price. This is how I work: one designer, fixed packages and a first brand board in 48 hours.

## How to choose

- **Want many ideas fast and cheap?** A contest (99designs or DesignCrowd).
- **Need a logo today for under $100?** An AI tool or a Fiverr gig.
- **Need ongoing design every month?** A subscription.
- **Need a complete identity you'll keep for years?** An independent designer or studio with a portfolio you love.

Whichever you choose, confirm three things in writing: the files you'll receive, the number of concepts and revisions, and that the copyright transfers to you.
