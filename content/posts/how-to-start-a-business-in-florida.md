Most guides on how to start a business in Florida stop at the paperwork. That's the easy part. The harder part is choosing a name and a brand you can keep, because a Florida LLC or DBA doesn't give you any rights to your business name.

**Short answer:** to start a business in Florida, pick a name and check it on Sunbiz, form an LLC ($125) or register a fictitious name ($50) if you trade under a different name, get an EIN, register for sales tax if you sell taxable goods, and get a local business tax receipt from your county and city. Then lock down your brand: a trademark search, a logo you own, and the basic files you'll need on day one.

Below is the full checklist, in the order I'd do it, with the branding steps most people forget.

## What are the steps to start a business in Florida?

Here's the order that saves you the most rework:

1. **Choose a name and check it's free.** Search Sunbiz, the USPTO trademark database and the domain at the same time.
2. **Pick a business structure.** Sole proprietorship, LLC or corporation.
3. **Register with the state.** Form your LLC or corporation on Sunbiz, or register a fictitious name.
4. **Get an EIN** (federal tax ID) from the IRS.
5. **Register for state taxes** if you sell taxable goods or services.
6. **Get your local business tax receipt** from your county and, if you're inside city limits, your city.
7. **Check professional licenses** if your trade is regulated.
8. **Open a business bank account.**
9. **Build your brand:** logo, colors, domain, email and the files your printer and sign maker will need.

Steps 1 and 9 are where most founders lose money, so I'll spend more time on those.

## How much does it cost to start a business in Florida?

The state fees are low. These are the figures I checked on the Florida Division of Corporations and the IRS:

| Step | Cost | Source |
|---|---|---|
| LLC Articles of Organization | $125 ($100 filing + $25 registered agent designation) | [Sunbiz LLC fees](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/forms/fees/llc-fees/) |
| LLC annual report | $138.75 a year; $538.75 if received after May 1 | [Sunbiz LLC fees](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/forms/fees/llc-fees/) |
| Fictitious name (DBA) | $50, valid 5 years | [Sunbiz fictitious name](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/start-business/efile/fl-fictitious-name-registration/) |
| Name reservation (optional) | $25 | [Sunbiz LLC fees](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/forms/fees/llc-fees/) |
| Florida trademark | About $87.50 per class | [UpCounsel](https://upcounsel.com/florida-trademark) |
| Federal trademark (USPTO) | $350 per class base fee | [USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/fees-payment-information/summary-2025-trademark-fee-changes) |
| Local business tax receipt | Set by each county and city | [Fla. Stat. ch. 205](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter205/All) |

Note the late annual report figure. Missing the May 1 deadline turns a $138.75 bill into $538.75, so put it in your calendar the day you form the LLC.

## Do I need a business license in Florida?

Florida has no general statewide business license, as [Harbor Compliance](https://www.harborcompliance.com/florida-business-license) explains. What most businesses do need is a **local business tax receipt**. Under [Chapter 205 of the Florida Statutes](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter205/All), counties and cities can each charge this tax for the privilege of doing business in their area, so a business inside city limits may need one from both. Receipts are due by September 30 each year, and operating without one can add a 25% penalty.

Some trades also need a state license (contractors, cosmetologists, real estate agents and others), so check with the relevant board before you open.

If you sell taxable goods or services, the [Florida Department of Revenue](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/registration) says you must register as a sales and use tax dealer **before** you start trading. The online Florida Business Tax Application walks you through which taxes apply. Florida's state sales tax is 6% on most goods, plus county surtaxes ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/how-to-start-a-business-in-florida)).

## Do I need a DBA in Florida?

You need a fictitious name registration if you trade under any name other than your own legal name (for a sole proprietor) or your exact registered name (for an LLC or corporation). Sunbiz's [fictitious name FAQ](https://efile.sunbiz.org/ficfaq.html) covers the rules:

- It costs $50 and expires on December 31 of the fifth year.
- You must advertise the name at least once in a newspaper in the county where your business is based. You certify that you did when you file.
- Banks and your local tax office usually ask for proof of it.
- Trading under an unregistered fictitious name is a second-degree misdemeanor under s. 865.09.

So if "Sunshine Coastal Cleaning LLC" trades as "Coastal Clean", that second name needs registering.

## Does registering my business name on Sunbiz protect it?

No, and this is the most expensive misunderstanding I see. Sunbiz says plainly that a fictitious name registration is for public notice only. It doesn't give you ownership of the name or stop anyone else using it. An LLC name works the same way: it only stops another Florida entity filing with the same name.

Your real protection is a **trademark**, and the risk runs both ways. If someone already uses a similar name for similar goods, your shiny new Florida LLC won't stop them from asking you to rebrand.

Your two options:

- **Florida trademark:** about $87.50 per class, covers use within Florida, lasts five years and renews for further five-year periods. It requires that you're already using the mark ([UpCounsel](https://upcounsel.com/florida-trademark)).
- **Federal trademark:** $350 per class base fee at the USPTO, with extra surcharges if you write your own description of goods instead of using the ID Manual ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/fees-payment-information/summary-2025-trademark-fee-changes)). It covers the whole country, which matters if you sell online.

Before you pay for anything, search the USPTO database and Google for your name in your industry. Ten minutes now can save you a sign, a van wrap and a website later.

## The branding checklist before you open

Once the name is clear, this is what a new Florida business needs before opening day:

- [ ] **A logo you own.** Get the copyright transferred in writing. Template and AI-generated logos are hard to protect; I explain why in [can you copyright an AI-generated logo](/blog/can-you-copyright-an-ai-generated-logo/).

- [ ] **Domain and business email** that match the name.
- [ ] **Google Business Profile** with your logo, hours and service area.
- [ ] **Day-one print:** business cards, a storefront or vehicle sign, invoices and social profile images.
- [ ] **Bilingual versions** if you serve Spanish-speaking customers. See [bilingual branding in Florida](/florida/bilingual-branding/).

## How long does it take to start a business in Florida?

The state filing is the quick part: you can file an LLC or fictitious name on Sunbiz online. The slower steps are usually local: the business tax receipt, any zoning check for a storefront or home business, and professional licensing. Plan for a few weeks in total, and start your brand work at the same time so you aren't printing signs with a temporary logo.

## FAQ

### What do I need to start a business in Florida legally?
A registered entity on Sunbiz (or a fictitious name if you trade under a different name), an EIN, a local business tax receipt, sales tax registration if you sell taxable goods or services, and any state license your trade requires.

### How much is an LLC in Florida?
$125 to file the Articles of Organization, then $138.75 for each annual report. File the annual report before May 1 or the fee rises to $538.75.

### Is a DBA the same as a trademark in Florida?
No. A fictitious name registration is public notice only and gives no rights to the name. A trademark, state or federal, is what protects your brand name and logo.

### Can I run a business from home in Florida?
Often yes, but you still need a local business tax receipt, and your city or county may have home-business rules. Check with your local tax collector before you start.

### Should I trademark my logo or my business name first?
Usually the name, because it's harder to change and appears everywhere. Many businesses file the logo later once the design is final. Ask a trademark attorney for your situation.
