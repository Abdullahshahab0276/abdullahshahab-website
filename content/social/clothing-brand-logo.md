## LinkedIn post
In most industries, the logo sits on the box. In fashion, the logo is often the product.

That changes how a clothing brand logo has to be designed. It needs to look good stitched on a cap, printed on a hoodie, woven into a neck label and shrunk to a profile picture.

A few rules from embroidery suppliers like Custom Ink, Printful and Rush Order Tees:

• Text at least 0.25 inch tall (about 36 pt)
• Lines at least 0.05 inch thick, or they become a single run stitch
• No gradients, halftones, neon or metallic effects in thread
• Three to six thread colors is plenty
• Hat logos have about 2 inches of height to work with

So every clothing brand needs two versions from day one: a full wordmark for big prints and packaging, and a short mark (a monogram or symbol) for caps, sleeves and labels.

And check the name early. A USPTO trademark application now starts at $350 per class.
