## LinkedIn post
Most construction logos are designed on a laptop and fail on a truck.

A contractor's logo lives on a pickup door, a hard hat, an embroidered vest, a dusty site banner and a bid sitting next to two competitors. That's a harder brief than most brands get.

What works:

• A bold, readable name. One second to read, from across a parking lot.
• One simple mark, not a crane, hammer and house together.
• Two or three solid colors that survive vinyl and embroidery.
• A compact icon version for hard hats and stickers.

Two rules many contractors miss:

• Commercial trucks covered by FMCSA rules must show the company name and USDOT number on both sides, readable from 50 feet in daylight (49 CFR 390.21T).
• In Florida, if a vehicle carries your contracting company's name or artwork, your license number has to be displayed with it (s. 489.119).

So design space for that lettering from day one, not as an afterthought.
